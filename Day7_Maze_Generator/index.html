How Randomized DFS Works 🧠

The Randomized Depth-First Search (also known as the "Recursive Backtracker") is one of the most popular algorithms for generating "Perfect Mazes".

Key Characteristics:

  • Perfect Maze: Every cell is reachable, and there is exactly one path between any two cells (no loops!).
  • Winding Paths: DFS tends to create long, winding corridors with fewer junctions compared to other algorithms like Prim's.
  • Dead Ends: It creates many dead ends, making the maze more challenging to solve manually.

The "Backtracking" Magic:

When the algorithm hits a dead end (a cell where all neighbors are already visited), it "backtracks" through its history (the Stack) until it finds a cell that still has unvisited neighbors. This ensures that every single corner of the grid is carved out.
